Uptempo vs rawstyle vs zaag: maybe you don’t know the difference yet, but that’s okay! Back in early 2024 they all used to sound very similar to me, so I didn't know the difference either. Early on I used to confuse bouncy uptempo with zaag, but luckily my brother was able to explain the difference to me whenever I asked him. However, after a few months I learned how to hear the differences. In this post I wrote a step by step guide based on my own experience, and a few extra tips to help you learn the difference.

## What you need before you start

- Spotify and/or SoundCloud. I learned almost everything by actively listening at home.
- Headphones are optional but preferred (or a good partybox speaker)
- Having friends in the scene also helps so they can explain the difference if you're ever confused.
- You'll need a bit of patience as well, a lot of tracks and kicks do sound similar, and it can be tricky to learn the difference since the kicks have so much distortion.

## Step 1: Uptempo vs rawstyle, let's start with the speed

A big difference between uptempo and rawstyle is the speed. Uptempo is usually a higher tempo than rawstyle. Although the scene is changing very rapidly, so the BPM (beats per minute) on average is also changing. Nowadays artists are commonly lowering the BPM in their uptempo tracks a bit.

## Step 2: What should you listen to first?

Even though I personally started by listening to bouncy uptempo, I would recommend starting with rawstyle, especially if you're still new to the scene.

Rawstyle is overall more friendly for beginners in my opinion. It's easier to dance and listen to than uptempo, because the kicks don't sound nearly as distorted. Rawstyle is more comparable with hardstyle and hard techno, which makes it more accessible to a wider audience. I've also noticed when bringing new people to raves that they usually want to spend more time at the rawstyle stages.

## Step 3: What is rawstyle?

Rawstyle evolved from the hardstyle scene and consists of heavy, deep, and dark sounding kicks that sometimes have a screech after them (somewhat similar to hard techno). To me it often sounds like metal being hit very hard.

A few examples of popular artists that usually produce these types of kicks are: [Sickmode](https://open.spotify.com/artist/5PbgCU02dfdBCAzpOaNmYW), [Rooler](https://open.spotify.com/artist/2lpFs8QJyIeVDb2Sq4vZYi), [Mish](https://open.spotify.com/artist/65kwwmTEJIlKRldGhmUM0b), [Riot Shift](https://open.spotify.com/artist/5J3RIw3YwgyXeosItjR9GY), [So Juice](https://open.spotify.com/artist/7MBMIGBbRcIAn4zZPOMNKR), [Anderex](https://open.spotify.com/artist/7wXBtpPB5GFddtz9OeBlPW), [Kruelty](https://open.spotify.com/artist/30sKm4Zacgq8mC0l7vNmuD), and [Exproz](https://open.spotify.com/artist/22jcDppDfbm4cqqUDzM8tU) (although Kruelty and Exproz lean a lot more towards hard techno).

At rawstyle stages you'll often see people doing the "klaplongen" move, because it works well with rawstyle.

### New and modern kicks: PVC and ping pong

Due to the scene changing rapidly there have recently been some newer types of rawstyle kicks that have gained attention. An example; PVC kicks, largely popularised by [Dual Damage](https://open.spotify.com/artist/05bETZtzSdUI5fconFIKRX). They got their name because they sound like hitting a PVC pipe.

Another example of new upcoming kicks is "ping pong" kicks. There's even a track that's called "[Ping pong battle (Infliction, Revelation, Hard Destiny)](https://open.spotify.com/track/3opYVBWYLtfDSBomdweQvt)". This track perfectly demonstrates these kicks, and as you can probably guess, these kicks sound like a ping pong ball.

Uptempo evolved from early hardcore, and gabber, [because of DJs like Partyraiser](https://www.hardcultr.com/guides/hardcore-vs-uptempo-explained-2026/). This genre has been gaining more traction in the past few years, especially bouncy uptempo.

Uptempo kicks are often more aggressive and distorted, whilst still being easy to dance to. The way I'd usually describe them is a rattling sound, something like "brrrrt".

Bouncy kicks are easier to do the classic Dutch "hakken" move to compared to rawstyle and zaag. Because uptempo evolved from the hardcore scene it also adopted the dance move.

### What are kickrolls?

Kickrolls are somewhat difficult to describe. They don't just apply to uptempo. Instead, they appear throughout the entire hardcore & hardstyle scene. The best way I can describe them is that they are the part of the track that forms a catchy melody. Usually a kickroll happens right after a drop. The kicks can sometimes go off beat, and some of the kicks might shift in pitch to make the track sound more interesting.

At raves you will always see people swinging their arms to match the beat (usually referred to as "hitting the kickrolls"). A nice example of someone hitting a perfect kickroll is shown in this video.

## Step 5: Zaag, the extreme side of uptempo

Zaagkicks are a subgenre of uptempo which sound even more extreme. The name is based on the Dutch word for "saw", because zaagkicks often sound like a chainsaw. Zaagkicks can also be divided into several different types. I would personally consider the two main to be Deutsche krach and microwave kicks.

They both sound similar, but can be differentiated. Deutsche krach is slower and deeper, while microwave kicks are more screechy and high pitched, and they have a harder "punch" at the start. This results in microwave kicks having stronger and more intense drops in my opinion. Occasionally people will even bring actual microwaves to festivals and bring them to the stage where artists like Invaderz are playing.

A few examples of artists for each type are:

- **Deutsche krach**: [Noiseflow](https://open.spotify.com/artist/7fJ280aOmeo3dA2KHeVxP3), [Dr. Donk](https://open.spotify.com/artist/0NirwTOv2qvuXsNNZkDPNu), [Schlot](https://open.spotify.com/artist/0XDU5Tj8CTvRZBaKB6pSSw), [vANE](https://open.spotify.com/artist/5ybO01zdAE3T5pqUxIGlns)
- **Microwave kicks**: [Invaderz](https://open.spotify.com/artist/1eKL2T8Hy0bjjjLWlxGQrD), [ALBINO](https://open.spotify.com/artist/0ZEaOlMqKyqXsHuLl3Su9b), [Equal2](https://open.spotify.com/artist/3gghhafHb61InwF1vnntja), [RWF](https://open.spotify.com/artist/5SSccKfaWK4yIu8TsqUtac), [Acidz](https://open.spotify.com/artist/4AflTeWUY89j7GYJvyWGAo)

### Zaag that doesn't fit a box (in my opinion)

There's also zaag that doesn't directly fall under those two categories. It is still quite extreme but not as extreme as those other two. Some of the bigger artists like [The Dark Horror](https://open.spotify.com/artist/3e7LfI39pUL22PeGn1mlFa), [Kemal](https://open.spotify.com/artist/7JW9vNFifByjqiuZMVwlwg), [Yoshiko](https://open.spotify.com/artist/4lSE8OyTMhErkE7OshR2Hq), and [Lekkerfaces](https://open.spotify.com/artist/45Meh6B3L1TNh03On3y349) play their own unique mix of bouncy uptempo and zaag.

I could honestly classify Lekkerfaces separately, since his kicks are another level of unique compared to others I've mentioned so far. Which is why he's my favorite DJ.

## Step 6: Test yourself at a real event

Speaking from experience I would say that these are a few good examples of festivals where you could learn the difference:

- REBiRTH: perfect blend of raw, uptempo, and zaag. Curious about REBiRTH? [Here's my honest REBiRTH 2026 review.](/blog/rebirth-festival-2026-review/)
- REBELLiON indoor: mostly raw and some uptempo
- Intents: mix of everything, even including hardstyle
- Dominator: mostly uptempo and zaag

Walk between the different stages and listen to the music. If you're still unsure try to see how people are dancing:

- Klaplongen at raw
- Hakken at uptempo
- No specific dance move at zaag. Most people will be kickrolling instead (You might occasionally see a microwave on stage, which is a great indicator that you're at the zaag stage.)

## Quick recap

- Listen for the speed first. Rawstyle is slower, uptempo is faster.
- Start with rawstyle if you're still new to the scene.
- Rawstyle: deep, heavy kicks that sound like hitting metal, sometimes followed by a screech. People will be doing the "klaplongen" move.
- Bouncy uptempo: Kicks that sound distorted and have a rattling sound. People will be doing the "hakken" move.
- Zaag: chainsaw kicks, mostly Deutsche krach or microwave.
