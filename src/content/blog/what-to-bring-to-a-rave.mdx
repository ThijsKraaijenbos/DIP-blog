If you're unsure about what to bring to a rave and what to leave at home, don't worry, I've been there as well. I've been to plenty of raves, so by now I know quite well what you should have on your list of items to bring. This year alone I've already been to 7 raves/festivals. This includes: Gearbox, REBiRTH, Friction, Intents, Dominator, and the first and second editions of CONFUSED. I've even got another rave already planned (2nd edition of Friction). However, even with my experience going to quite a few raves, I still somehow manage to sometimes forget something. That's exactly why I've written this post so you can avoid forgetting your important items. By providing my own list of things I'd recommend to bring, and items I'd recommend leaving at home, you'll be able to be fully prepared for your next rave!

At the end there's also a short checklist that you can screenshot before you go. (New to the music? I wrote about how I learned to hear the difference between uptempo, rawstyle and zaag [in this post](/blog/uptempo-vs-rawstyle-vs-zaag/).)

## Why does it matter what you bring?

Most big festivals are pretty strict about what you can bring into the festival. At the entrance there's usually a ticket check first, and at the second check the security searches you. Anything that isn't allowed simply gets taken or thrown away. At most big festivals (like REBiRTH for example) you also can't leave the festival grounds and come back in later (see [REBiRTH FAQ](https://www.rebirth-festival.nl/en/faq/)). So if you forget something you'll just have to deal with it for the rest of the day.

If you don't think about what to bring there's a high chance that you'll end up regretting not bringing certain items. A few things I regretted the most were not bringing a warm jacket or other comfy warm clothes for the ride home, not bringing a cap for my water bottle, and not bringing a powerbank. During the day it's usually very warm and you'll be dancing the whole time, but once you're waiting for your ride at night it can often get cold very quickly, so you'll want a comfy jacket for that. And since these festivals almost always take the caps off of the water bottles you'll be stuck holding an open bottle for the rest of the day if you don't bring one yourself.

## What to bring to a rave: the 9 things I always bring

### 1. Your ticket and ID

This sounds obvious, but it's one of the main things you absolutely shouldn't forget. Make sure you personalise your ticket beforehand and download it, or save a screenshot in case you don't have cell service or internet at the entrance. You should also always bring your ID. Even though not everyone gets asked to show it, almost all festivals are 18+, and even at 16+ raves you're occasionally asked to show ID. So if you get asked and you don't have it with you, you won't be able to get in.

### 2. Earplugs

The music does get very loud at these events, especially when you're close to the speakers. I simply use some cheap earplugs that I bought at a festival roughly two years ago (I'm honestly surprised I somehow haven't lost them yet), and they work fine for me. If you don't have any yet, you can usually buy them on the festival grounds. If you ever experience ringing ears after a rave, it's a sign that your hearing is already slightly damaged (see [Unity: Hearing damage](https://unity.nl/en/drugs-gebruiken/gezondheid/gehoorschade/)), and that damage can become permanent. So it's very important that you protect your ears with earplugs!

(Some of my friends recommended Loop earplugs to me. I haven't used them myself, so take it with a grain of salt, but they seem to be good from what I've been told. This is not a promotion or advertisement for them.)

### 3. Your phone and a powerbank

Your phone is an important one, even though it's best to be in the moment and not be on your phone, you'll need it for your ticket and for communicating with friends if you've lost them. I'd highly recommend bringing a powerbank to make sure you never end up in a difficult situation where you can't communicate with your friends. Especially if you use your phone often to take videos and pictures, because that can drain your battery quite a bit. It can also be useful for your friends if they forget to bring one but still end up needing one.

*Tip: Appic has an interactive map of the festivals for most large festivals. If you ever lose your friends at the festival you can also see where they are if you have them as friends on Appic.*

### 4. Money for coins

At most big festivals you can't pay with cash or card at the bars. Usually you have to buy coins. A strip of 5 coins costs roughly 20 bucks at most festivals. Want to know what a full day costs me on average? I wrote it all down in my [REBiRTH 2026 review](/blog/rebirth-festival-2026-review/).

### 5. A jacket for the ride home

This is the one I regretted the most. During the day it's warm and you're dancing a lot, so you won't need a jacket at all. But at the end of the night when you're walking back to the car or waiting for your bus, it gets cold very quickly. So just to be safe you should bring one and put it in a locker during the day.

You don't need a separate locker for each person's jacket either. I usually buy the cheapest locker option, and if you squeeze hard enough you can fit 2 or 3 people's jackets and bags in there. Lockers are almost always right next to the entrance, so you can put your stuff away straight after you get in.

### 6. A cap for your water bottle

This one might sound a bit weird, but festivals pretty much NEVER give you the cap when you buy a bottle of water. So you either have to finish it right away or keep holding an open bottle while you're dancing, which gets very annoying. That's why I've recently started bringing a spare cap from an empty bottle at home, so I can simply close it and save the rest for later.

*Tip: online you can buy reusable bottle caps for festivals. If you search for "festicap", "partycap", or "festidop" you should be able to find them. Additionally, if you have a 3D printer it's worth mentioning that there are 3D models of these bottle caps online that can easily be 3D printed.*

### 7. Comfortable shoes

You're going to be walking and dancing all day, and the festival grounds are usually quite big. So wear a pair of shoes that are comfortable and that you've already walked in a lot, not a brand new pair that you're wearing for the first time. Recommended shoes for raves are AirMax BW, or AirMax 90s. They make it easier to do the "hakken" dancemove, and they are very stereotypical within the Dutch hardcore scene. (If you're interested in the history behind the AirMax shoes within hardcore culture you can read more [here](https://thehundreds.com/blogs/content/gabber-dutch-subculture-air-max-bw))

### 8. A foldable fan

A handheld foldable rave fan is an absolute lifesaver for both indoor and outdoor raves. Especially for hot days at outdoor festivals, and in general for indoor raves because those tend to be hotter, especially if the ventilation isn't great. Curious about the differences between indoor vs outdoor raves? Check out [my post](/blog/indoor-vs-outdoor-raves/) where I compare them in detail. These hand fans can often be bought at merch stands. If you want to buy one online I would recommend buying a strong one made of bamboo or wood with a strong fabric, because the plastic ones unfortunately tend to break very easily.

### 9. Make sure your ride home is sorted

This is a very important one. Before you go, always make sure you know exactly how you're getting home, because figuring that out during the festival with an empty battery is not fun. Especially if you live far away. There's a few options:

- Carpool with friends. This is almost always cheaper than a partybus or public transport.
- Take a partybus. You can find partybus routes on [Eleventravel](https://eleventravel.nl/evenementen/rebirth-festival-zondag/?search=rotterdam), which usually start at big stations like Rotterdam Central station and take you directly to the entrance of the festival grounds.
- Take public transport. A lot of these hardcore festivals take place in North Brabant. Usually there will be shuttle bus options from cities like Eindhoven, 's-Hertogenbosch, or Tilburg. [(Intents for example has options for all three of those)](https://www.intentsfestival.nl/busvervoer/). Make sure to read the festival's official FAQ or travel options pages so you can plan your route.
- Ask someone to pick you up. If a parent or a friend is coming to get you, ask them to bring some snacks and drinks for the ride home.

[Celebrate Safe](https://www.artofdance.nl/celebrate-safe/) also recommends planning how you're getting home before you go, and never driving after drinking.

## Small extras (nice to have)

These aren't necessary, but they can make your day a bit more comfortable. Most of them are mainly for outdoor festivals. Not sure if you'd rather go to an indoor or outdoor rave? I compared both [in this post](/blog/indoor-vs-outdoor-raves/), check it out if you're interested!

- Sunglasses. I usually bring them to outdoor festivals.
- Tissues and some gum.
- A poncho if you know it's going to rain. Sometimes they even give out free ponchos, like they did at Intents this year.
- A small crossbody bag. I recently ordered one myself, I haven't brought it to a rave yet though.
- Snacks and drinks in the car for the ride home.

## What I'd leave at home

### 1. Your own food and drinks

You can bring some snacks and drinks for the ride to the festival, but you aren't allowed to bring them inside. So make sure to finish it or throw anything away that you still have left before you go through security. If you're carpooling with friends I'd recommend leaving some snacks and drinks in the car for the ride home.

### 2. Spray deodorant and perfume

These aren't allowed at festivals (see [REBELLiON FAQ](https://www.jointherebellion.nl/en/faq/)), so security will take them from you at the entrance. Just put some on at home before you leave instead. You'll probably be smelling like sweat at the end of the festival anyway if you're dancing all day, so it doesn't really make a difference.

### 3. An umbrella

Umbrellas usually aren't allowed either (see [REBELLiON FAQ](https://www.jointherebellion.nl/en/faq/)). If you know it's going to rain, bring a poncho instead. You can sometimes buy them at the festival as well.

### 4. Jewelry and other expensive stuff

It's very easy to lose these things in a big crowd. Jewelry also overall gets quite uncomfortable to wear, especially on hot days, so it's better to just leave it at home.
